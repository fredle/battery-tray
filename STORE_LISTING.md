## Product name

Battery Tray

## Short description

See the battery level of your Bluetooth headphones, mouse and keyboard in the tray, and get a warning before one runs out.

## Description

Windows keeps the battery level of your Bluetooth devices several clicks deep in Settings, and says nothing when one is about to run out. You find out when the mouse stops or the headphones go quiet in the middle of a call.

Battery Tray puts the number in the system tray. Pick a device and its battery percentage is drawn right into the tray icon. Hover over the icon to see every paired device, and get a warning when a battery falls to the level you set.

The battery level of every device is free, with no trial that runs out. The tray icon and the low-battery warning are free for one device you choose. An optional one-off unlock (£2.99, bought on our website) adds low-battery warnings for every device.

What it reads: Bluetooth LE devices that have the standard Battery Service, and classic Bluetooth devices whose level Windows already knows.
What it does not read: separate levels for each earbud and the case, wired devices, and devices on a 2.4 GHz USB receiver.

Useful details:
- One card per device with its name, its level and whether it is connected.
- A device that is switched off shows its last level and how long ago that was.
- A device that does not report its battery says so. The app never guesses.
- The warning appears once per discharge, not every minute.
- Closing the window keeps the app in the tray. Starting with Windows is one tick.
- Checks about once a minute in the background, so the window never freezes.

About accuracy: the number comes from the device itself, through Windows. Some headphones only report in steps of 10 or 20, and a few report nothing at all. Battery Tray shows exactly what the device reports.

No account, no ads, no telemetry.

## Privacy

Stored locally in %LOCALAPPDATA%\BatteryTray: settings.json (tray device,
warning threshold, language, licence key) and devices.json (each paired
device's name, Bluetooth address, last battery level and when it was read).
Nothing else is kept.

Network: a version check against GitHub Releases about once a day, which the
user can turn off. No device names, addresses or usage data are sent anywhere.

Feedback: the "What would make this better?" link in the window lets you send the developer a short note. Only the note, the app's name and version, and the platform are sent, to the developer's server at feedback.leatham.net, and only when you press Send. No name, email address or device identifier is sent, and the server does not keep IP addresses. Notes are used only to improve the app and are never shared or sold.
